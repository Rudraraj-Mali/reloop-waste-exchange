Lossy vs. Lossless Compression

⚡ Lossy (JPEG, WebP)

Permanently removes data the eye won't notice. Achieves 60–90% reduction. Best for photos, social content, and blog images.

  • ✓ Huge file size savings
  • ✓ Still visually excellent at quality 80+
  • ✗ Slight quality reduction vs original

🔒 Lossless (PNG)

Preserves every pixel perfectly. Smaller savings (10–30%). Best for logos, UI screenshots, and transparent images.

  • ✓ Zero quality degradation
  • ✓ Perfect for text & sharp edges
  • ✗ Larger files than lossy for photos

Why Image Compression Matters

Images account for over 50% of the average webpage's total bytes. Unoptimized images are the #1 cause of slow page loads, high bounce rates, and poor Core Web Vitals — which directly affect your Google rankings.

Google's Lighthouse specifically flags uncompressed images as a critical issue. Properly optimized images improve your Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) score — one of Google's top ranking signals.

A 5 MB hero image can add 5–8 seconds of load time on mobile. Compressed to 300 KB it loads in under a second. Research shows a 1-second delay reduces conversions by 7%. Smaller images also lower CDN bandwidth costs and improve experience for users on slower connections worldwide.

What Is WebP and Should You Use It?

WebP is a modern format by Google that supports both lossy and lossless compression, transparency, and animation — beating JPEG by 25–35% and PNG by 26% at equivalent quality. As of 2024, WebP is supported by 96%+ of all browsers including Chrome, Firefox, Safari 14+, and Edge. For most web images, WebP at quality 75–85 is the clear best choice.
